## 6.1 Where MS02 sits

MS02 is the second member server, reachable only through the SOCKS tunnel from chapter 05. It is the
host that makes the rest of the chain plausible: administrators log on to member servers, so a
privileged session or a reusable local credential is far more likely to be cached there than on
MS01, whose LSASS held nothing domain-tier (chapter 04).

## 6.2 How it will be documented

The chapter will cover, in order:

1. **Discovery** — the `10.10.118.0/24` sweep through proxychains, the host's name and OS, and which
   ports answered (`445`, `5985`, `3389` are the ones that matter on a member server).
2. **Access** — the credential or technique that opened it, and why that credential existed at all.
3. **Local enumeration** — who is logged on, what the token holds, and whether the host is a
   dead end like MS01 or holds the next hop.
4. **The artefact** — the hash, ticket, or credential that unlocks DC01, and the check that proves it
   is domain-tier before using it.

## 6.3 Commands that carry over

Everything from chapter 05 applies unchanged; only the target address changes:

```bash
proxychains4 -q nxc smb 10.10.118.0/24
proxychains4 -q nxc winrm 10.10.118.0/24 -u <user> -p '<password>'
```

Anything that needs a real network stack (LSASS dumps, SharpHound, RDP) runs **from MS01** or from
MS02 itself, not through the proxy.
